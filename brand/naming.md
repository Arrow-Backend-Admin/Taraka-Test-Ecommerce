# Sāntvana — Name Risk Memo

**Phase 1 deliverable · v1.1 · 30 October 2026**
Status: **Not a clearance opinion. Concept work may proceed; print, domain and ad spend are blocked until the attorney opinion in §6 is back.**

---

## 1. What this document is

Sāntvana (सांत्वना; also spelled सान्त्वना) replaced the previous name after a Class 25 collision risk. This memo supersedes the v1.0 memo on that name. It records what Sāntvana means, how to say and spell it, the connotation risk that has to be tested with native speakers, the similar marks I am aware of, unchecked domain and handle ideas, and the verification steps.

It does not clear the name. Nothing here should be read as "available", "safe" or "registrable". Those are conclusions for a trademark attorney, not for a brand document.

## 2. The name

| Form | Use |
|---|---|
| **Sāntvana** | Latin display, brand assets, editorial |
| **सान्त्वना** | Accepted dictionary spelling — conjunct न्त्व; not the logo form; search/legal only |
| **सांत्वना** | Devanagari lockup — anusvara spelling; **selected for the logo, 30 Oct 2026** |
| **Santvana** | ASCII fallback: domains, handles, email, invoices, legal, filenames, hashtags |
| **SANTVANA** | Trademark search and filing representation (ask the attorney) |

Both Devanagari forms are the name. Neither is a misspelling; dictionaries use both. The logo uses the form the founder selects, and search plus legal review cover both.

**Meaning.** From Sanskrit सान्त्वन (sāntvana): consolation, solace, comfort; the act of soothing or pacifying someone in distress. In modern Hindi, सान्त्वना / सांत्वना is the ordinary noun for consolation — the comfort you give someone who is grieving or disappointed. The brand is using the word for the feeling of being looked after, not for the act of pitying someone.

**Pronunciation.** **SAAN-tvuh-naa.**

- **sā** — a long "aa" as in "father", lightly nasalised.
- **ntv** — the cluster n-tv, pronounced "n-tv" with a light "uh" after the v. In the conjunct spelling it is written न्त्व; in the selected anusvara spelling the nasal is the dot in सांत्वना. The **t** is dental, softer than the English t.
- **nā** — a long "aa" again.
- Stress the first syllable. Do not say "san-TVAH-na" (middle stress) in brand film, voiceover or retail training.
- Common English approximations: "SAANT-vuh-nah", "SAHN-tvuh-nah". Pick one and use it everywhere; the macron is a spelling mark, not a different pronunciation.
- The name is three syllables in Hindi, not four.
- The full transliteration is **sāntvanā**. The final long ā is pronounced even though the Latin display form does not mark it; see §4 for the display decision.
- Both Devanagari spellings are pronounced the same: the anusvara in सांत्वना assimilates to n before t.

**Script note.** The Devanagari is not a translation; it is the primary form. The Latin is a transliteration. That matters for the logo hierarchy in Phase 2: the Devanagari lockup is not an optional accessory. The logo uses **सांत्वना** (anusvara), selected on 30 October 2026. **सान्त्वना** remains an accepted dictionary spelling for search, legal review and pronunciation, but it is not the logo form.

## 3. Connotation risk — the main issue

This is the risk that matters more than trademark for the first impression.

- **Grief and consolation.** In everyday Hindi, सांत्वना / सान्त्वना is often used for consoling someone after a death or a loss. "सांत्वना देना" / "सान्त्वना देना" is to console. A footwear brand that leads with the word may read as solemn, religious or bereavement-adjacent before it reads as comfort.
- **Consolation prize.** "सांत्वना पुरस्कार" / "सान्त्वना पुरस्कार" is a common phrase for a consolation prize. That is the opposite of what a footwear brand wants to signal: second-best, awarded after you lose.
- **Register.** The word is Sanskrit-derived and slightly literary. Some speakers will find it beautiful and precise; others will find it formal, old-fashioned or "filmy". It is not a neutral everyday word like "comfort".
- **Wellness drift.** Because the word is used in counselling and charity contexts, it may read as a mental-health or spiritual brand. That is not fatal — the category is comfort — but the visual identity must not lean into lotus, hands, candles or pastel wellness cues.
- **Regional variation.** Hindi is not the first language of every target customer. In Bengaluru, Chennai, Hyderabad and parts of Mumbai, the word may be understood but not felt. Test in more than one language community; do not assume a Hindi-first reaction is the national reaction.

**How to test it (do this before any spend):**

1. Recruit 15–20 native Hindi speakers across at least three cities (for example Delhi, Lucknow and Mumbai) and two age bands (22–34 and 45–60), plus 5 non-Hindi speakers from the target market.
2. Do not show the logo or the category first. Show both **सान्त्वना** and **सांत्वना**. Ask: "Are these the same word? Which looks more familiar? What does it mean to you?" Then: "What kind of brand would use this name?" Then: "Have you seen it before? Where?" Record any mention of the NIMHANS palliative-care service or orphanages. Then: "Say it aloud." Then show the shoe.
3. Record verbatim answers. Count how many volunteer grief, consolation prize, religion, palliative care, orphanage or wellness without prompting.
4. Test the Devanagari lockup at 16, 20, 24 and 32 px (see `strategy.md` §5).
5. If the grief or consolation-prize association is the first or only meaning for most respondents, the name is a strategy problem, not a copy problem. Do not try to fix it with a tagline.

## 4. Spelling policy

- **Display decision, stated once:** the Latin brand form is **Sāntvana**, with the macron on the first ā only. The final long ā is **not** marked in Latin. The Devanagari carries it, and the pronunciation guide says -naa. One diacritic keeps the wordmark legible at small sizes and keeps the ASCII fallback unambiguous.
- **Devanagari:** सांत्वना (anusvara) is the logo form, selected 30 October 2026. सान्त्वना (the alternative conjunct spelling) remains an accepted dictionary spelling for search and legal review; both are the name. All Phase 2 legibility and size rules apply to **सांत्वना**, not the conjunct spelling.
- **Size floor:** **20 px on screen** and **6 mm measured wordmark height in print**. The screen test covers 1×, 1.5× and 2× DPR at 40/255 and 128/255 alpha. At 20 and 24 px on 1×/50% alpha the full-word त्व counter closes; both pass at DPR ≥ 1.5. On 1×, use 28 px.
- **Below 20 px:** use the Latin monogram or symbol, never the Devanagari wordmark. This covers favicons, app icons, avatars, UI labels and any placement under the floor.
- **Re-run trigger:** re-run `logos/legibility-test-anusvara.md` after the final Devanagari typeface is chosen. Nirmala UI is only a stand-in.
- **ASCII:** Santvana — domains, handles, email addresses, invoices, GST and legal documents, filenames, UTM tags, hashtags.
- **Trademark filings:** ask the attorney whether to file **SANTVANA** in Latin characters, **सांत्वना** and/or **सान्त्वना** in Devanagari, or both, and whether the macron changes the representation. Do not assume one filing covers the other.
- Never mix the two forms in one URL, handle or invoice. The macron is a typographic mark; search engines and registries will treat the ASCII form as the practical identifier.
- Hashtags use ASCII: **#SantvanaFootwear**, not #SāntvanaFootwear.
- On first use in a campaign, "Sāntvana (SAAN-tvuh-naa)" is acceptable. Do not repeat the pronunciation on every page.

## 5. Similar marks and real-world associations

This is not a search report. It is a list of things to put in front of the attorney.

**Santana-formative footwear and fashion marks.** I am aware of Santana Canada (cold-weather footwear) and a Carlos Santana licensed footwear line. Both are in or adjacent to Class 25. They are spelled differently (Santana vs Santvana), pronounced differently (san-TAH-na vs SAAN-tvuh-naa), and carry a Latin-music or surname association rather than a Hindi one. They matter because they are in the same class and the same broad channel; they do not settle the question. The extra "v" and the different stress pattern are arguments, not a clearance.

**"Santvana" in wellness, counselling and NGO names.** I expect to find Indian mental-health, counselling and charitable organisations using Santvana or Sāntvana. Different classes, but they affect search results, social handles and the "what does this brand do?" reaction. Verify; do not assume.

**SANTVANA palliative-care service at NIMHANS, Bengaluru.** A palliative-care service using the name SANTVANA is a real-world association to test. Palliative care is comfort care for serious illness, so the association is semantically close to the brand's "solace" meaning — and close enough to make the name read as a hospice, hospital or charity. Test it cold: show the name without the shoe and ask what organisation they think it is. If respondents know the NIMHANS service, record whether that helps or hijacks the brand. Do not use the association in marketing.

**Orphanages named Santvana.** Orphanages using Santvana are a second real-world association to test. They reinforce the care/charity meaning and may make the name read as an NGO or a donation appeal rather than a footwear brand. Test the same way. Verify current names and locations before citing them anywhere public; I have not independently verified them.

**Devanagari device marks.** A stylised सान्त्वना or सांत्वना, or a similar Devanagari wordmark, may exist even if the Latin word is free. Search both Devanagari forms separately; transliteration is not automatic in every database.

**Phonetic neighbours.** Santaan, Santvani, Saanvi, Saanjh and Santosh are not the same mark, but they crowd voice search, app-store search and spelling over the phone. Check the ones that matter for paid search.

**What I cannot tell you:** whether any of these is registered in India, whether it is live, whether it would be cited against you, or whether a co-existence argument would work. That is what §6 is for.

## 6. Verification steps — do these before any spend

1. **IP India public search** — <https://ipindiaonline.gov.in/tmrpublicsearch/frmmain.aspx>. Search `SANTVANA`, `SANTVANA` stylised, both Devanagari forms `सान्त्वना` and `सांत्वना`, and phonetic/transliteration equivalents (`SAANTVANA`, `SANTVANAA`) in **Class 25** (footwear) and **Class 35** (retail services). Record application numbers, status, goods specification and proprietor. Repeat for device marks.
2. **TMview** — <https://www.tmdn.org/tmview/> — and **WIPO Global Brand Database** — <https://branddb.wipo.int/>. Filter to India, Classes 25 and 35; check Madrid designations.
3. **Devanagari search** — run both `सान्त्वना` (conjunct) and `सांत्वना` (anusvara) separately in IP India and WIPO; do not rely on transliteration.
4. **WHOIS** — `santvana.in`, `santvana.co.in`, `santvana.co`, `santvana.com`, `santvanafootwear.com`, `wearsantvana.com`, `santvana.fit`. Use an accredited registrar and <https://lookup.icann.org/>. A premium listing is not availability.
5. **Social handles** — Instagram, X, Facebook, YouTube, LinkedIn, Pinterest: `@santvana`, `@santvanafootwear`, `@santvana.in`, `@wearsantvana`, `@santvanaofficial`. A free handle today is not a reservation; register what you need the day you decide.
6. **Native-speaker connotation test** — §3. Do this before paid media, packaging print or a launch film.
7. **Attorney opinion** — an Indian trademark attorney, written, covering Class 25 footwear and Class 35 retail. Ask specifically: (a) registered marks that would be infringed; (b) pending applications that could support an opposition; (c) the Santana-formative marks; (d) whether to file Latin, Devanagari or both; (e) the macron's effect on the representation; (f) whether a co-existence argument is realistic.
8. **Do not spend** on packaging print, signage, paid media, incorporation under the name, or app-store listings until step 7 is back.
9. **Re-run steps 1–5 immediately before launch.** Registers change and applications publish late.

## 8. What would make me stop

- A live Class 25 registration for SANTVANA or a confusingly similar mark covering footwear.
- A Devanagari device mark that dominates the same trade channel.
- Native-speaker testing where the grief or consolation-prize association is the first or only meaning for most respondents.
- An attorney opinion that says the risk is not manageable.

If any of these land, stop Phase 2 production on the name. Re-run the fallback exercise; the v1.0 shortlist (Vane, Fletch, Tir) was not cleared either, so it is a starting point, not a safety net.
